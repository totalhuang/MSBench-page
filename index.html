Overview

Text-to-video models now produce plausible short clips, but a multi-shot video needs more: shots that change when the script says, joined by the transition it asks for, filmed with the requested camera language, and consistent across cuts. MSBench measures how far this can be controlled from text. Each prompt is a structured Scene–Entity–Shot mini-script that fixes every shot's time span, camera state, content and outgoing transition, and each generated video is scored on four dimensions with 11 sub-metrics.

One script, several systems

Pick a script. The upper strip under each video is the timeline the script requests; the lower strip shows the transitions our detector found in the generated video. Click a strip to seek.

Leaderboard

15 commercial and open-weight systems, all evaluated by us on the 250-script base set (4s + 8s); the ten that natively generate 15-second video are also scored on the 50-script 15s extension. Scores are 0–100, higher is better; Final is the equal-weight mean of the four dimensions, and its system ranking reaches a Spearman correlation of with human pairwise preference. Click a header to sort, tick models to compare them. The four dimensions are explained under Evaluation.

Findings

Visual quality does little to separate the 14 systems: on the same script, two systems differ by 7.9 points in Aesthetic Score but by 23.0 in Semantic Alignment. What separates them is how they execute the script. The paper reports five findings; browse them below.

Evaluation

Specialist perception models are paired with VLM reasoning, and one detected shot segmentation is shared by every metric. For each sub-metric below, the same script is shown with a higher-scoring and a lower-scoring generation.

Dataset

300 mini-scripts at 4, 8 and 15 seconds, spanning 15 topics and two to six shots. Scripts vary along two axes: whether an entity recurs across shots, and whether the shots share one scene. Hover any chart for exact counts.

Submit a model

We run the evaluation ourselves, so every leaderboard entry is produced by the same pipeline. You send videos; we send back scores and update the leaderboard in periodic rounds.

  1. Generate. One video per script, from the provided t2v_prompt or from the structured fields adapted to your model's input format.
  2. Name and host. Save each video as <hash>.mp4 and share the folder from Google Drive, Hugging Face or Baidu Netdisk.
  3. Submit the link. Through the Google Form or the mainland-China entry. We evaluate and update the leaderboard in periodic rounds.
